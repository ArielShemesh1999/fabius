## 1 · The positioning canvas

Fill every field before writing a word of copy. A blank field is a question for the user (→ `fabius-disciplina`), not a guess — vague positioning is where every downstream word goes soft.

| Field | How to fill it | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | the ONE segment that feels the pain most acutely — named, not "everyone" | a persona so broad it fits nobody |
| **Pain** | the problem in *their* words, scraped from reviews / support tickets / sales calls | your internal jargon for it |
| **Category** | the shelf they already shop — the frame they file you under in one beat | inventing a category they have to learn |
| **Outcome** | the single after-state they're buying (the verb, not the feature) | listing features instead of the result |
| **Alternative** | what they use *today* — often a spreadsheet, a manual habit, or nothing | naming a competitor they've never heard of |
| **Difference** | the one thing you do the alternative can't — provable, not adjectival | a claim a rival could paste onto their own page |

**The one-sentence output** — fill it; if it doesn't hold, you're not ready to write:

```
For [who] who [pain], [product] is the [category] that [outcome].
Unlike [alternative], it [difference].
```

Everything downstream — headline, hooks, CTAs, the whole funnel — inherits from this sentence. Re-derive nothing; this is the source.

## 2 · Match the message to the awareness level

A reader arrives at one of five levels (Schwartz). Lead with the level the channel actually delivers. Pitch the product to the product-aware; pitch the *problem* to the problem-aware. Writing product copy at an unaware audience is the most common waste.

| Level | They know | Lead with | Concrete lead-line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | nothing — not even the problem | name the problem for the first time | "You re-answer the same five support tickets every morning." |
| **Problem-aware** | the pain, not that fixes exist | agitate it, then promise a path | "Still triaging tickets by hand at 9am? There's a faster way." |
| **Solution-aware** | approaches exist, not which | why *yours* wins the comparison | "Most ticket bots need a 3-week setup. This one reads your old replies and works today." |
| **Product-aware** | you exist, weighing it | offer · proof · price | "Resolve 60% of tickets on autopilot. 1,200 teams. Free for 14 days." |
| **Most-aware** | the details, deciding | the deal + the deadline — get out of the way | "Your 14-day trial ends Friday. Upgrade now, keep your bot." |

Channel delivers the level: cold ad ≈ unaware/problem · search ≈ solution · retargeting & email ≈ product/most. Cold traffic ≠ your warm list — never write the same line at both.

## 3 · Proof over adjectives — the honest-claim rule

Replace every claim with the evidence under it. An adjective a competitor can copy for free is not a differentiator; the figure, the demo, and the named testimonial are. (Same discipline as fabius's own benchmark posture — lead with the proven line, never the inflated one; `fabius-disciplina`.)

| Soft claim | Proof that replaces it |
|---|---|
| "Blazing fast" | "Renders in 0.3s — measured, p95" |
| "Loved by teams" | a named logo, or the verbatim quote + role |
| "Easy to set up" | "Live in 4 minutes" + the 12-second demo clip |
| "Trusted by thousands" | "3,214 teams" — the exact number; round numbers read like guesses |
| "Save time" | "Cuts triage from 2h to 15min/day" |
| "Best-in-class" | the benchmark table, the rank, the source |

If a claim won't survive scrutiny, it costs more than the conversion it buys. Cut it.

## 4 · The converting structures — pick ONE, don't stack

Each below is a fill-in template. Choosing two and interleaving them is how copy turns to mush.

### PAS — Problem · Agitate · Solution

Default for cold, problem-aware traffic. Best for ads, cold email, top-of-landing.

```
[PROBLEM]  You [the specific recurring pain, in their words].
[AGITATE]  And it's not just [surface cost] — it's [the deeper cost:
           time / money / the thing they actually fear].
           Every [day/week] it [compounds].
[SOLUTION] [Product] [does the one thing that ends it].
           [One proof line]. [One CTA].
```

### AIDA — Attention · Interest · Desire · Action

Long-form landing pages, sales pages, webinar pitches.

```
[ATTENTION] [Headline: the bold, specific promise — see §5]
[INTEREST]  [Why it's possible / what's broken about the old way]
            [2–4 concrete proof beats — numbers, demo, names]
[DESIRE]    [The after-state, vividly: a day in their life, fixed]
            [Testimonial that mirrors the reader]
[ACTION]    [One CTA, restated. Risk-reverser: "free / cancel anytime"]
```

### BAB — Before · After · Bridge

Transformation offers. Pairs with a demo or screenshot diptych.

```
[BEFORE] Today you [the painful status quo — concrete, dated].
[AFTER]  Imagine [the after-state — same scene, transformed].
[BRIDGE] [Product] is how you get from one to the other:
         [the single mechanism]. [Proof]. [CTA].
```

### The one-thing email

One ask, one proof, one link. No menu. The whole-email discipline lives in [channel-swipe-library.md](channel-swipe-library.md); the skeleton:

```
Subject: [one specific promise or curiosity gap — see swipe file]
[One line of context — why you, why now]
[The one ask, stated plainly]
[One proof line OR one reason it's worth it]
[One link / button — the only clickable thing]
[Sign-off]
```

Two asks in one email ≈ no ask. If a second link is "also useful," it's a tax on the first.

## 5 · Headlines — the 4-U test + the formula bank

The headline carries ~80% of the result. Spend the time here.

**The 4-U test.** Score 1–4 on each; a weak headline fails one outright. Specific beats clever every time.

| U | Asks | Fix if weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Useful** | does it promise a clear value? | name the outcome, not the feature |
| **Urgent** | why read it *now*? | add the cost of waiting, or a deadline |
| **Unique** | could only you say it? | swap a generic claim for your difference |
| **Ultra-specific** | a number, a named outcome, a concrete noun? | replace "more/better/fast" with a figure |

**Formula bank** (fill the brackets; the deep bank lives in the swipe file):

```
How to [outcome] without [the dreaded cost]
[Number] ways to [outcome] — even if [common objection]
The [adjective] way to [outcome] (and why [old way] fails)
[Achieve outcome] in [specific timeframe]
Finally, [outcome] for [specific who]
Stop [painful action]. Start [desired action].
[Product] is the [category] that [the one difference].
You don't need [expensive/hard thing] to [outcome]. You need [your thing].
What [aspirational group] know about [topic] that you don't
[Do hard thing] in [short time] — here's the exact [method]
```

One idea per headline. A headline doing two jobs does neither.

## 6 · Objection handling — surface it, then disarm

Unspoken objections kill conversions silently. Name the top three the reader is thinking, answer each on the page. The moves:

| Move | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-empt** | a predictable doubt ("too expensive", "too hard") | state it before they do: "Think this needs a dev team? It doesn't —" |
| **Reframe** | the objection rests on a false frame | "It's not a cost, it's the hour/day you get back" |
| **Proof-swap** | "does it actually work?" | replace reassurance with a number, demo, or named user |
| **Risk-reverse** | fear of being stuck/burned | free trial · money-back · cancel anytime · "keep the export" |
| **Smallest-yes** | the ask feels too big | shrink it: "no card", "just see the demo", "2-minute setup" |
| **Honest-no** | it genuinely isn't for them | "If you [edge case], this isn't your tool" — disqualifying builds trust |

The objection rebuttal swipe lines are in [channel-swipe-library.md](channel-swipe-library.md).

## 7 · The funnel is `surface → one next step`

Every surface earns **exactly one** next action. Name it, then delete every competing CTA — a second button taxes the first. Two asks on one surface ≈ no ask.

| Stage | Surface | Awareness it meets | The one next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | ad · social post | unaware / problem | **click** (one hook, one promise) |
| Land | landing / sales page | problem → product | **the one CTA** (signup OR buy OR book — pick one) |
| Nurture | email sequence | product / most | **return / convert** (one ask per send) |
| Convert | checkout · booking · trial start | most | **complete** (strip every distraction, no nav) |
| Expand | onboarding · upsell email | post-purchase | **activate** (the one action that proves value) |

Map the path end to end, put one job on each step, and make each step point at the next.

## 8 · Measure one metric per stage

Tie the test to the stage. A landing-page rewrite is judged on conversion, not impressions.

```
ad / post   → click-through rate      (is the hook landing?)
landing     → conversion rate         (is offer + proof enough?)
email       → reply / click rate      (is the one ask right?)
checkout    → completion rate         (is friction killing the yes?)
onboarding  → activation rate         (did they reach the value moment?)
launch      → the single north-star tied to the step you're testing
```

One metric per stage. Watching all of them at once means optimizing none of them.

## 9 · The launch loop — smallest campaign that tests the claim

Don't build the funnel; test the message. Scout wide on angles, strike narrow on the one that moves the number.

```
[ ] 1. Lock positioning (§1). One sentence holds, or stop and ask.
[ ] 2. Pick the ONE core claim to test (the riskiest assumption).
[ ] 3. Ship the smallest asset that puts it in front of real traffic
       (one ad · one landing · one cold-email batch — not the funnel).
[ ] 4. Define ONE metric for that stage (§8) and a kill/scale threshold.
[ ] 5. Run it small. Read the number, not your hopes.
[ ] 6. Iterate the MESSAGE before scaling the SPEND.
       Win → scale that angle. Loss → new angle, same asset. Repeat.
```

**When NOT to optimize** (`fabius-parcus`):
- **Don't A/B below significance.** Under thin traffic a "winner" is noise — ship the stronger-reasoned version and move on.
- **Don't polish copy on an unvalidated offer.** Great words on a thing nobody wants is wasted craft — validate demand first (→ `fabius-disciplina`).
- **Don't claim what you can't prove** (§3).

## RTL / Hebrew note

RTL surfaces (Hebrew, Arabic) keep their own reading direction and rhythm: the eye enters top-**right**, so the CTA, the logo's primacy, and the visual hierarchy mirror. Don't force an English left-to-right layout onto an RTL page — flip the grid, the icon direction, and the line-start, and let numerals/Latin runs stay LTR inside the RTL flow. Hook punch lands at the right edge, not the left. (Visual execution and the `dir` mechanics are `fabius-decor`; this layer owns that the *message* reads natively, not translated-feeling.)
